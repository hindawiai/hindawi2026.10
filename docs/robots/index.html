Build and drive the robot

Two robots, the same commands. Stage 1 is an Arduino UNO with a motor shield, driven over its USB cable. Stage 3 is an ESP32, driven over WiFi from any phone. You program both in your own language. Each step says what you should see; if you see it, go on to the next.

Ages: Stage 1 from 12, Stage 3 from 16, with an adult present. The basic robot is meant to cost under 1000 rupees; prices differ by seller. Try the commands first in the robot simulator: ayecnse.net (sims.ayecnse.site), part of the AyeAI triad (icansee.life). The control interface the robots answer is ayecnse/ayeam-robot.

2. Put it together

  1. Fix the two motors and the caster to the chassis, and the battery pack where it will not move.
  2. Stage 1: press the L293D shield onto the UNO. Left motor to M1, right motor to M2. Battery to the shield's power terminals (red to +).
  3. Stage 3: wire the ESP32 to the L298N as in the table below; the L298N takes the battery; join the L298N's ground to an ESP32 GND pin.
ESP32 pinL298NDoes
26IN1left motor forward
27IN2left motor backward
14IN3right motor forward
12IN4right motor backward
25ENAleft motor speed
33ENBright motor speed
GNDGNDa common ground: without it nothing moves

On the L298N, take off the ENA and ENB jumpers so the ESP32 sets the speed.

3. Load the program

  1. Install the Arduino IDE. For Stage 1 add the Adafruit Motor Shield library (Tools, Manage Libraries, search AFMotor). For Stage 3 add the board package esp32 by Espressif (Tools, Board, Boards Manager).
  2. Open the sketch, in English or in your language: robot_coding/uno_l293d_usb/ or robot_coding/esp32_l298n_wifi/, and the same in urdu/, hindi/, telugu/, kannada/, tamil/, punjabi/.
  3. For a sketch in your language, connect the IDE to Hindawi once, from a copy of hindawiai/hindawi2026.10: bash ide/arduino/install.sh --lang urdu (your language in place of urdu), then restart the IDE.
  4. Stage 3: write your WiFi name and password in the sketch (2.4 GHz networks only).
  5. Choose the board (Arduino Uno, or your ESP32 board) and its port, and press Upload.
You should see: "Done uploading". Open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. Stage 1 prints READY; Stage 3 prints robot at http://192.168.….

4. The first test

Lift the robot so its wheels are off the table. Every command is three values: direction, time in milliseconds, speed from 0 to 255. Direction is F, B, L, R or S.

Stage 1: in the Serial Monitor, with the line ending set to "Newline", type F,1000,200 and press Enter.

You should see: both wheels turn forward for one second, then stop, and the Serial Monitor shows OK.

Stage 3: on a phone joined to the same WiFi, open the address the Serial Monitor printed. The robot's own page opens: arrows, a stop button, speed and time. Press the up arrow.

You should see: both wheels turn forward for the time set, and the page shows F: OK.

6. Before you conclude it does not work

Almost every robot that "does not work" is a power or wiring matter, not the program. Check these in order:

  1. Is the program answering? If the Serial Monitor or the phone page shows OK after a command, the program works; what remains is power or wiring.
  2. Power: fresh or charged batteries, connected the right way round, switched on. USB power alone does not turn the motors.
  3. Stage 1: the shield pressed fully onto the UNO; motors in M1 and M2.
  4. Stage 3: the common ground wire; the ENA and ENB jumpers removed; 2.4 GHz WiFi; the phone on the same network.
  5. One wheel turns the wrong way: swap that motor's two wires. Nothing is wrong with the program.
  6. Nothing in the Serial Monitor: the baud rate is 115200; the right port is chosen; the line ending is "Newline".
  7. The sketch in your language does not compile: run the Hindawi connection step again for that language; the English sketch beside it compiles without it.

7. Record what happened

Each run you report becomes evidence for everyone who builds the next robot. Note: the board, the sketch and its language, the command, and what the robot did. Open an issue on hindawiai/hindawi2026.10 with the title "Robot run: Stage 1 (or 3), language", and a photo or a short video if you can.

8. Safety

Build and drive with an adult present. Batteries, chargers and motors can heat up or catch fire if shorted: never leave a charging battery unattended, and keep fingers clear of the wheels when testing.
